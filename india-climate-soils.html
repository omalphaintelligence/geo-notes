Indian climate: dry winters & dry summers

Tropical monsoon climate shaped by relief + latitude; a clear rhythm of seasons. IMD seasons: dry winter (15 Dec–15 Mar), dry summer (15 Mar–15 Jun), advancing monsoon (15 Jun–15 Sep), retreating monsoon (15 Sep–15 Dec).

Dry winters
  • Low‑sun season; stable air, offshore winds → dry.
  • High pressure over India = sub‑tropical high, about 1014–1019 mb.
  • Country mostly dry, but the sub‑tropical westerly jet brings western disturbances to the NW – roughly the area bounded by 22°N and 82°E.
Dry summers
  • Sun and thermal equator move north; the 42 °C isotherm shifts from south to north; by June all India is hot.
  • Peninsula (maritime) vs extra‑peninsula (continental) contrast of >10 °C.
  • Weak pressure gradient draws local moist winds → pre‑monsoon showers: tea showers (sub‑Himalayan WB & Assam), mango showers, cherry blossom (Karnataka).
  • Kalbaisakhi (nor'westers) – LP over Hooghly plains + moisture → violent storms with heavy rain and sea surges.
  • Loo – hot dry wind from Thar–plains pressure contrast; heat stroke up to Rohilkhand plains. Strong loo carries desert dust; scattering gives some relief from extreme heat.
  • Hailstorms in northern plains; local heat waves and cyclones.
Cross‑check: notes place mango showers in Maharashtra; standard texts – Kerala & coastal Karnataka.

Advancing & retreating monsoon

Arabian Sea branch

Pressure contrast pulls SE trades in as the SW monsoon. AS branch: rain falls south → north and west → east in the peninsula.

Advancing monsoon gives >70% of annual rain – except Coromandel coast (winds parallel to coast), Lahaul–Spiti and Leh–Ladakh (continentality + relief).

Retreating (NE) monsoon
  • Sun moves south, HP returns over land, NE trades re‑established; pick up moisture over warm BoB → up to 200 cm on Coromandel coast. In dry winters NE trades blow but BoB is too cool to give rain.
  • Tropical cyclones – second rain mechanism. BoB more prone (continentality). Lower Gangetic plain & Utkal coast more hit than Coromandel.
  • Arabian Sea: Sahyadris shield Konkan; Malabar and Gujarat coast more prone – Gulf of Khambhat most cyclone‑prone; Gulf of Kutch almost free (salt marshes heat less, no LP).
  • October heat – hot, humid, oppressive weather in interiors from waterlogged land after good rains.

Indian biomes (latitudinal)

MoEF: Indian biodiversity mainly tropical; differences driven by rainfall. 5 biomes – latitudinal: tropical evergreen, tropical deciduous, tropical thorny; altitudinal: sub‑tropical montane, Himalayan.

Tropical deciduous & thorny
  • Tropical deciduous – largest area (monsoon climate). Moist (150–200 cm): Gangetic plains, Utkal, Narmada–Tapi, Bangalore–Mysore tableland – sal, teak, sandalwood. Dry: semi‑arid belt Punjab–Gujarat, plateau interiors – teak, sal, palas, kendu.
  • Fauna: elephants, tigers, lion (Gir), chital, sambhar, bustards (Bengal florican, lesser florican, MacQueen's), flamingoes, gharials, dolphins.
  • Tropical thorny – deserts & drought‑prone interiors (W Rajasthan, N Gujarat, Sahyadri rain‑shadow, Telangana, Dandakaranya, Chota Nagpur). Xerophytes: babul, Senegal acacia, sabai grass; halophyte Kabuli kikar. Fauna: chinkara, great Indian bustard, blackbuck, vulture, desert fox, flamingoes.
Cross‑check: notes give ~150 cm for dry deciduous; standard range is ~70–100 cm.
Sub‑tropical montane (southern hills)
  • Variation mainly from maritime influence. Nilgiris, Anaimalai, Cardamom – west slope windward (advancing monsoon). Javadi & Shevaroy – east slope windward (retreating monsoon).
  • Windward side: rain falls with height; lee side: rain falls as height drops – biggest contrast at the piedmont.
  • Zonation: moist evergreen / dry evergreen (<700 m) → moist deciduous (700–1400 m) → wet montane (>1400 m); lee side dry deciduous and thorny.

Himalayan biome & zoogeographic zones

Himalayan biome

Highest biotic diversity due to height; only the southern sun‑facing slope counts.

NE Himalaya

Sikkim, Arunachal; strong maritime effect – moist evergreen & moist deciduous. Red panda, lesser cats, elephant, rhino; Sikkim stag in temperate heights.

Trans‑Himalaya

J&K, lee of Himadri; low productivity, grazers – gazelle, ibex, yak; brackish lakes host black‑necked crane.

NW Himalaya (most diverse)

Tropical deciduous (<1500 m: sambhar, goral) → temperate deciduous (1500–3300 m: brown bear, koklas, musk deer) → coniferous/taiga (3300–4500 m: snow leopard, wolf). Treeline ~4500 m; alpine tundra to 6000 m; frost above.
Zoogeographic zones

By Wildlife Institute of India – Rodgers & Panwar: 10 zones – Trans‑Himalaya, Himalaya, Desert, Semi‑arid, Western Ghats (with southern hills), Deccan, Coastal, Gangetic plains, NE states, Islands (Nicobar = equatorial biome). Biodiversity is highest where warm + wet.

Cross‑check: Rodgers & Panwar classification is usually dated 1988 (notes 1986).

Environment protection: forest conservation

MoEF is the nodal ministry; 3 flagship laws: FCA 1980, Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Environment Protection Act 1986.

Forest status (2014 report)
  • India 10th most forested, ~1.8% of world forest area; stable despite population pressure.
  • ~23% of area forested: forest 20% + tree cover 3%. Order: moderately dense > open (incl. mangroves) > very dense.
  • Mountains hold ~39%; NE states (except Assam) >75% forested; Punjab–Haryana <10%.
Cross‑check: these are 2014 figures; recent ISFR puts forest + tree cover near 25%.
Indian Forest Act 1927 & FCA 1980
  • 1927 Indian Forest Act – first comprehensive, purely regulatory, no participation.
  • FCA 1980 – stops diversion of forest land; compensatory afforestation by user agency in cash/kind.
  • After Supreme Court intervention, user agency pays for land + destroyed forest resource; in protected areas 5–10 times net present value plus SC approval.
  • Money held by national CAMPA advisory council outside the Consolidated Fund, in the name of state CAMPAs; released as annual plan funds. Up to 5% for monitoring → e‑Green Watch (AP, Karnataka, MP, Sikkim, Tripura).
NFP 1988, FRA 2006, REDD+, NEP 2006
  • National Forest Policy 1988: from regulatory → participatory, revenue → conservation. JFM guidelines 1990; social forestry now linked with MGNREGA.
  • Forest Rights Act 2006 – ends "historical injustice" to STs and other traditional forest dwellers.
  • REDD+ (India, 2009 per notes) – pays for reducing deforestation plus conservation, sustainable management and more forest cover; involves forest dwellers.
  • National Environment Policy 2006 – mangrove (open forest) + coral reef protection via IUCN's Mangroves for the Future (MFF) (~5% quality mangroves; priority WB, Gujarat, A&N) and CRZ notification 2011 (priority Sundarbans, Navi & Greater Mumbai, Goa, Kerala, A&N).

Wildlife & biodiversity protection

Wildlife Protection Act 1972
  • Integrated development of wildlife habitat; 3 aims: support to protected areas, biodiversity protection, recovery programmes.
  • PAs (2003 amendment) by number: wildlife sanctuaries (fully natural) > national parks (semi‑natural) > conservation reserves > community reserves.
  • Critically endangered animals in Schedule/category I – highest protection via Wildlife Crime Control Bureau; fines & jail for hunting tigers, lions, elephants, gharials, antelopes, rhinos.
  • Funding: 100% central for non‑recurring items, and for PAs in mountains, deserts, coasts and with endangered species; otherwise recurring costs shared equally Centre–state.
  • Global recognition: MAB biosphere reserves (notes: 8 of 18 in UNESCO list) and natural World Heritage Sites (notes: 6) – Western Ghats (incl. Radhanagari & Chandoli) added 2012; Achanakmar–Amarkantak BR in MAB list.
Cross‑check: counts are from 2014; more Indian BRs are now in the MAB list (12 by 2020s).
Biological Diversity Act 2002
  • In line with CBD; National Biodiversity Authority (autonomous, under MoEF) → State Biodiversity Boards → Biodiversity Management Committees at panchayat level.
  • National strategies from 2008: G‑SHE (Governance for Sustaining Himalayan Ecosystem), GANGES (global advisory network involving NRIs/PIOs for support), TEEB (Economics of Ecosystems & Biodiversity; Like‑Minded Megadiverse Countries – 17 countries, ~70% of global biodiversity).
Recovery programmes

Flagship

Project Tiger and Project Elephant (1992); separate national authorities; WWF support. Tigers survive in only ~7% of original range; India has >50% of Asian elephants. MIKE (Monitoring Illegal Killing of Elephants, under CITES). Largest TR: Nagarjunasagar–Srisailam; highest elephant density: Bandipur.

Non‑flagship

Project Vulture, Project Gharial – captive breeding; Project Hangul (Kashmir stag, a red deer sub‑species), Project Snow Leopard – location‑specific; Project Cheetah – reviving a species extinct in the wild in India.
Cross‑check: Project Tiger was launched in 1973 (notes 1972).

Environment Protection Act 1986 & climate change

Climate change mitigation & pollution control with the Central Pollution Control Board. Two key activities: INCCA and NAPCC.

INCCA
  • Indian Network for Climate Change Assessment – "4×4" assessment for 2030: regions (Himalayas, NE states, Western Ghats, coasts) × sectors (agriculture, water, forests, health).
  • Also: National Carbonaceous Aerosol Programme (chemistry, time variation and spectral effect of aerosols on heat budget), Montreal Protocol compliance, EIA.
Montreal Protocol compliance
  • From Vienna Convention; India a consistent, successful developing country. India produces 9 of 96 listed ODS.
  • Multilateral fund support; ODS Rules 2000; CFC & CTC production/consumption frozen by 2010 (minor pharma exception); HCFC (interim substitute) freeze year 2030.
EIA, CREP, CEPI
  • EIA notification 2006 – "green certificate" mandatory for big projects replacing habitat or in dense forests; 2009 amendment exempted biomass/biogas power plants; state‑level EIA authorities set up.
  • CREP (Charter on Corporate Responsibility for Environmental Protection) – 15 highly polluting industries: metallurgical, petrochemicals, paper‑pulp, pharma, fertiliser.
  • CEPI (Comprehensive Environmental Pollution Index, CPCB) – scale 0–100; >70 = critically polluted, no new green certificate; 43 such clusters notified.
NAPCC missions

Missions under various ministries (notes list 9): National Solar Mission (JNNSM), Enhanced Energy Efficiency, Strategic Knowledge, Sustaining Himalayan Ecosystem, Green India, Water, Sustainable Habitat, Sustainable Agriculture, Clean Coal (notes).

Cross‑check: NAPCC was released in 2008 with 8 missions (notes say 2009, 9 missions).

Geological structure: Archean, Purana, Dravidian

GSI: 4 systems – Archean (Gneiss & Schist, Dharwar), Purana (Cuddapah, Vindhyan), Dravidian (Haimanta), Aryan (Gondwana, Deccan Trap, Recent). Archean + Purana = Azoic.

Archean system (4000–1400 Ma)
  • Gneiss & Schist series – Indian shield from original cooling; metamorphosed plutonic; basement complex, "backbone of the Himalayas"; covers >2/3 of peninsula. Little economic value except muscovite mica (Koderma); biotite mica at Nellore. Surface outcrops: Bengal series (Chota Nagpur), Bundelkhand series, Charnockite series (southern hills).
  • Dharwar series (upper Archean) – oldest sedimentary rocks; metalliferous.

Purana system (1400–600 Ma)
  • Fully sedimentary. Cuddapah (lower) – formed with sea transgression → metallic + non‑metallic. Largest in AP: Kurnool, Cuddapah, Anantapur (Ramagiri gold) – manganese, dolomite, gold. Largest manganese node: Nagpur & Bhandara, Bhilai & Durg, Balaghat. Also copper Balaghat; dolomite Seoni, Jabalpur, Raipur, Bilaspur; iron Dalli–Rajhara; limestone Bundi, Sawai Madhopur, Kota.
  • Vindhyan (upper) – mainly non‑metallic; as extensive as gneiss but buried under Deccan basalt and plains; shows as Vindhyan Range. Limestone – Satna, Rewa, Katni; diamonds – Panna (MP) and Golconda (AP, lacustrine).
Dravidian system (600–300 Ma)

Paleozoic; extra‑peninsular (Himalayan material from the peninsula); least studied. Haimanta series (Cambrian): rock salt (Pir Panjal per notes) and anthracite coal at Kalakot (J&K).

Cross‑check: India's Cambrian rock salt is usually linked with the Salt Range (Pakistan) and Mandi (HP).

Aryan system & earthquake zones

Gondwana series (coal)

Upper Carboniferous–Permian; coal‑bearing; mostly bituminous; in peninsular river basins (exception: Brahmaputra basin – Sibsagar). Coal = >50% of commercial energy; almost all with Coal India Ltd except Singareni (SCCL).

Deccan Trap & seismic highways
  • Indian plate moving north created transform faults → basic lava between upper Cretaceous and Eocene; covered older rocks; original height >1800 m; denuded into regur (black soil).
  • Major: Maharashtra, Gujarat; transitional: Karnataka, MP; minor: Chota Nagpur, Meghalaya, parts of TN. Explains lack of commercial minerals in Maharashtra.
  • Transform faults = seismic highways: Delhi–Haridwar, Aravalli, Narmada, Munger, Kurduwadi, Koyna, Malabar, Bhadrachalam, east coast.

Recent series (Pliocene–Pleistocene)
  • Formation of the plains and Rajasthan erg; Karewas of Kashmir (glacial deposits) – best for saffron.
  • Oil – Barmer; gas – Barmer, Tanot (notes also Kishangarh); lignite – Jaisalmer, Palana; Rann of Kutch – salt, lignite at Umarsar.
  • Monazite sands (Kollam–Kottayam) – world's richest thorium; Neyveli lignite (TN).
  • Oil: Ankleshwar, Kalol (Gujarat); Digboi, Duliajan, Naharkatiya (Assam). Gas: Krishna–Godavari basin. Offshore: Hazira, Rawa, Mumbai High (ONGC).

Indian soils (ICAR)

Diversity of relief, climate, vegetation and rocks → diverse soils. ICAR: alluvial, black, red, forest, desert, laterite, saline‑alkaline, peat/bog.

Alluvial
  • Largest area; notes: rich in nitrogen, humus, potash; low in phosphorus.
  • Structure: bhabar (coarsest cone), bhangar (old alluvium), khadar (new alluvium).
  • Sutlej–Yamuna plains are pedocal flood plains – more fertile than Brahmaputra pedalfer plains.
  • Extra‑peninsula deltaic soil only with Hooghly. Peninsula: west‑coast deltas coarser, east finer; Godavari–Krishna black alluvium more fertile than Kaveri–Mahanadi red alluvium; Narmada–Tapi lack alluvium (no deposition).
Cross‑check: standard texts: alluvium is rich in potash but generally poor in nitrogen, phosphorus and humus.
Black (regur)
  • Most fertile structural soil, on basalt; rich in lime, magnesium, iron, potash; little phosphorus; poor in nitrogen & humus; high water retention.
  • Dark – Maharashtra, Gujarat (most fertile); medium – Karnataka, MP; light – Shillong plateau, east Chota Nagpur, TN plateau.
Red & laterite
  • Red – on Archean rocks; most of peninsula; silica + aluminium + iron; technically infertile but responds to inputs – millets, pulses, oilseeds. Red‑yellow (thorny biome: Aravalli, Sahyadri rain‑shadow, Telangana, Dandakaranya, Chota Nagpur); red‑brown (deciduous biome: W Ghats, Bangalore–Mysore, E Ghats).
  • Laterite – on Archean base in per‑moist areas; leaching + laterisation → acidic; Malabar, windward Meghalaya. Infertile but commercially fertile for plantations – rubber, spices, coffee.
Desert, forest, saline, peat

Desert

Climate‑controlled; coarse, mineral‑rich, humus‑less; W Rajasthan (Jaisalmer, Jodhpur, Nagaur, Barmer, Bikaner); farmed with Indira Gandhi Canal; NDDB rates it best for fodder.

Forest (montane)

Humus & N‑rich, well drained, shallow; northern mountain wall; podzolic, podzol, chestnut; karewas, doons, duars; food crops and horticulture.

Saline‑alkaline

Halomorphic, infertile; Rann of Kutch salt marshes; salt‑tolerant fodder with NDDB.

Peat / bog

Infertile; only soil with no commercial use; tarai and littoral areas.
