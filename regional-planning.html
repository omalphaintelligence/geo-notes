Regions & regional geography

Whittlesey Committee (1956) – 3 regions
RegionNatureDelimitation
FormalHomogeneous (natural or human traits); spontaneous; well-defined geographic spaceQualitative + quantitative methods
FunctionalStrong economic/functional interdependence; node–periphery (urban agglomeration, industrial cluster, port hinterland); spontaneousQuantitative methods; "cascading systems"
PlannedInduced growth in areas lacking capacity; special type of formal region; not spontaneous, can be scatteredNo extra method – "controlled systems"

Region = phenomenon + area.

Regional geography & disparity
  • Regional approach = find causes of disparity and ways to reduce it. Regional synthesis overlaps with planning via place, folk, work.
  • Inequalities come from natural plus economic, political, social factors.
  • Models: non-spatial – Rostow; spatial – growth pole (Perroux, Myrdal, Isard & Perloff) and growth centre (Boudeville, Hermansen, R.P. Mishra).

Rostow's stages of growth (non-spatial)

Five stages
StageEconomyPolitics
1. Traditional societyPrimitive, subsistence agriculture, minimal income, rituals over rational enquiry"Nations"
2. Pre-conditions for take-offDiffusion of education, new middle class, selective productive investmentCentralised state
3. Take-offTrue momentum, diversified investment by state; lasts 20–30 yrs; roads, railways, agro-industriesCentralised state
4. Drive to maturityDiversified investment, holistic development, global links—
5. Mass consumptionMarket/consumer oriented investmentWelfare state

First three stages – developing countries; last two – developed. Rostow said developing countries will repeat the path of developed ones.

Limitations
  • Economic environment differs; markets now highly competitive – developing world can't simply repeat the path.
  • One economy can be in more than one stage at once.
  • Selective growth in take-off contradicted: China and India attempted diversified, balanced investment after independence.

Growth pole & growth centre (spatial)

Growth pole
  • Theory: growth pole = abstract (lab-oriented) economic space; growth centre = absolute geographic space. In practice synonyms.
  • Francois Perroux (1955): development starts at nodes with variable intensity and spills over; propulsive industry – one new/enlarged industry creates secondary industries, complexes, regions.
  • Myrdal: cumulative causation & spatial interaction; "initial kicks" (locational, demographic, economic) start poles; then trickle-down to neighbours.
  • Isard & Perloff (1962): export-based model – propulsive industry not compulsory; raw materials of national/international value earn revenue → investment; raw-material exporting countries as growth nodes.
Cross-check: notes date Myrdal's idea 1956; his book Economic Theory and Underdeveloped Regions is from 1957.
Growth centre
  • Boudeville (late 1960s): first growth centre concept – specific geographic location; urban multiplier model (urban areas have higher functional capacity); multiplier ≠ trickle-down.
  • Hermansen: regional multiplier model – rural areas sustain urban growth through backward (raw material) and forward (market) linkages.
  • R.P. Mishra: removed pole/centre distinction; hierarchy – growth pole (megacity) → growth centre (cities, diversified manufacturing) → growth point (towns, basic agro-manufacturing) → service centre (villages, primary activities).
Critics
  • Approach assumes trickle-down can end disparity.
  • N. Smith: support from capable to less capable regions doesn't remove diversity.
  • Lutgers: complex world order – disparity is reality; homogeneity impractical.
  • Friedmann (strongest critic): rejects trickle-down; bottom-up regional planning.

Regional planning in India & rural development

Approach
  • Socialist orientation; bottom-up since 1st Plan (notes). Wardha Plan: integrated agricultural development → integrated area development.
  • Area strategies: Backward Area Development (economic, cultural, mainstreaming, unfavourable nature) and Water Resource Management.
  • Backward-area approaches: rural, tribal, island, hill area development.
Rural development schemes
  • ~69% population rural. IRDP (notes: 5th Plan) – holistic rural development; 1981 Census shows controlled urbanisation. PURA to bridge rural–urban gap.
  • Bharat Nirman (2005) – planning + implementation + monitoring, bottom-up: rural housing, electrification, drinking water, roads, CIT, irrigation.
ComponentKey points
Housing – Indira Awas YojanaSC/ST, freed bonded labour, disabled, BPL; from Apr 2013 higher ceiling, 82 LWE districts priority, AWAASOFT; 75:25 (NE 90:10); via PRIs
Electrification – RGGVY (2005)REC (Ministry of Power); subsumed Kutir Jyoti (100% subsidy for BPL); MNRE off-grid, solar lamps (NAPCC); 2006 policy – 1 unit/household/day lifeline

Cross-check: IRDP began 1978–80 (6th Plan nationwide); Indira Awas Yojana launched 1985, independent scheme 1996.

Tribal area development

ST population & categories
  • ~8% of population; contiguous areas; NE states highest share, central India largest number; no ST in NCT, Chandigarh, Punjab, Haryana, Puducherry.
CategoryExamples
PrimitiveHunting-gathering: Raji (UP), Maria (CG), Yenadi (AP), Kadar & Puliyan (TN), Juang (OD), Shompen (Nicobar), Jarawa (Andaman). Nomadic herders: Todas, Raikas & Kathodias (RJ), Gaddis (HP), Bakarwals (J&K). Shifting: Lohitas (Arunachal)
ProgressiveAgriculture, political awareness: Bhils, Gonds (MP, CG); Santhal, Munda, Ho (JH); Bhumij (WB); Bhotias (UP); Garasiyas
DevelopedMainstream: Meenas (RJ), Negis (HP), Nagas, Mizos
Panchsheel & plans
  • Panchsheel: develop on own lines (nothing imposed); respect rights to land & forest; train/group their own people; collaboration not rivalry; judge by human character, not statistics.
  • 5th Plan: Tribal Main Plan (ST-majority states/UTs – NE states, Lakshadweep, Dadra & NH) and Tribal Sub-Plan (rest). 6th Plan: TSP split into ITDP (blocks/tehsils with substantial ST) and MADA (scattered primitive groups).
  • Protective: 5th Schedule (scheduled areas) & 6th Schedule (tribal areas) – land transfer to non-tribals barred; autonomous districts (notes: Bodo, Cachar – Assam; Garo, Khasi, Jaintia; Mara, Chakma – Mizoram).
  • Developmental: RTE, mid-day meal, distance education, post-matric scholarships; vocational/skills; NSTFDC and TRIFED; bamboo declared a "liberated item".
Cross-check: notes place Garo, Khasi, Jaintia councils in Nagaland – they are in Meghalaya.

Island & hill area development

Island territories
  • A&N and Lakshadweep: unique ethnicity, backwardness; A&N tribes – Andamanese, Jarawa, Nicobarese, Shompen (Negroid & Mongoloid).
  • Threat: sea-level rise (up to 0.9 m by 2100) – coastal arable land and Lakshadweep atolls.
  • From 6th Plan: integration with mainland – eco-tourism (beach, coastal, wildlife), sea links, Pawan Hans seaplanes, Port Blair & Kavaratti airports, cruise route; MSMEs (bakery, sea-shell, aquaculture processing, coconut oil, coir). Agriculture: aquaculture (A&N), coconut (Lakshadweep).
Hill area development
  • Challenge mainly physical. 5th Plan – ecological sustenance; 8th Plan – economic development & retaining working-age people.
  • Ecological: land-use planning (SoI, NRSA, NATMO – steep slopes forest, moderate horticulture, gentle seasonal crops); crop & animal zonation (Gaddis, Bakarwals – quality not quantity of livestock); soil & water (community check-dams).
  • Economic: eco-, adventure, spiritual tourism (limited by political sensitivity, unorganised services); agro-industries – horticulture processing (HP, Sahyadris), timber (Uttarakhand), tea (sub-Himalayan WB, Assam).

Water resources: flood & drought prone areas

India water-surplus (5th in surface water) but uneven in space & time. Programmes: flood-prone, drought-prone, command area, water resource management, watershed management.

Flood-prone area programme (5th Plan)
  • Flood = brief rise in water level; flooding (ICID) = overflow onto land not normally submerged. NDMA causes: monsoon rain, cyclonic surges, tsunamis; deforestation, embankment breaches.
CategoryAreasAvg duration
Most frequent & intenseBrahmaputra valley, Ganga plains4–6 weeks
ModerateBoB shoreline, Narmada & Tapi basins2–4 weeks
LeastSutlej–Yamuna plains, Malabar<2 weeks
Special (11th Plan)Urban floods – infrastructure planning—
  • Flood-proofing: precautionary (National Flood Commission – embankments, danger marks; biotic & concrete embankments, MFF mangroves, social forestry), predictive (IMD – 7 river forecast centres: Bhubaneswar–Mahanadi, Patna–Ganga, Lucknow–Gomti, Delhi–Yamuna, New Jalpaiguri–Teesta, Guwahati–Brahmaputra, Surat–Tapi; cyclone radars at Kolkata, Paradip, Vizag, Chennai, Bhuj, Kochi, Mumbai), rescue & rehabilitation (NDMA + army; rehabilitation most challenging).
Drought-prone area programme (5th Plan)
  • Hydrological, climatological, agricultural drought – agricultural most frequent; whole country except >200 cm rain is prone; semi-arid areas worst (highest rain variability).
  • Strategies: prediction (IMD), mitigation (dryland farming), rehabilitation (NDMA) + command area development. Dept of Agriculture also involved.

Command area development & watershed management

Command area development (5th Plan)
  • Water management began 1948 with DVC (first multipurpose project); CAD from 5th Plan. Aims: land & water management, food security, reduce regional disparity.
  • Size: major ≥20,000 ha CCA; medium 2,000–20,000 ha; minor <2,000 ha. Usually intra-basin; exception Indira Gandhi Canal (Indus water to Luni basin, from Harike barrage).
  • IGC: bajra/nomadic Raikas → wheat, groundnut, moong, olives, fodder (NDDB), tourism (Kathodias camel safari), industries at Bikaner, Nagaur, Jodhpur, Barmer, Jaisalmer.
  • Lower Kaveri: two rice crops, cane, cotton; textiles, tanneries, metallurgy – Mettur, Tiruppur, Salem, Trichy.
  • DVC Damodar command: drought-prone red-soil Chota Nagpur; silvi-pasture; core-sector industrial region.
Problems
  • (Planning Commission / CWC) Low water-use efficiency 20–30% – evaporation, percolation, siltation, weeds, broken structures; tail-enders suffer salinity & waterlogging.
  • Waterlogging – percolation raises water table; diversion reduces recharge elsewhere.
  • Funding: Union for major/medium; institutional finance for minor; from 11th Plan NABARD RIDF. Maintenance ≈ construction cost; no private players; Participatory Irrigation Management + NRM under MGNREGA.
Watershed management
  • FAO: unit where surface runoff flows in one direction. 1960s Catchment Area Development (Central Soil & Water Conservation Institute) in deltaic lowlands; mid-1970s water-divide management in drought-prone & desert areas.
  • After Earth Summit 1992 both merged as Watershed Management Programme; manages first-order streams; planned at Gram Panchayat level; PIM integral.

Map lists

  • Major ports: Kandla, Mumbai, Nhava Sheva, Mormugao, Mangalore, Kochi, Tuticorin, Ennore, Chennai, Visakhapatnam; dry ports Delhi, Bengaluru (mark hinterlands).
  • Railway zones: Konkan Railway Roha–Ratnagiri–Mangalore; Central (Mumbai CST), Western (Churchgate), Eastern (Kolkata), Hajipur, Bhubaneswar, Delhi, Allahabad, Jaipur, Gorakhpur, Maligaon, Chennai, Secunderabad, Hubli, South Eastern (Kolkata), Bilaspur.
  • NHDP: Golden Quadrilateral; N–S corridor Srinagar–Jhansi–Seoni–Kochi (notes; actually to Kanyakumari); E–W Silchar–Siliguri–Gorakhpur–Jhansi–Udaipur–Porbandar; J&K NH 1A, 1D (Srinagar–Kargil–Leh), 1C.
