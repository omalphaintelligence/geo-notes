Population theories: Malthus & Marx

Malthusian concept
  • Thomas Robert Malthus – "Essay on the Principle of Population". Two universal laws: passion between the sexes; food needed for survival.
  • Early marriage → more births. Preventive checks (human): delayed marriage, moral restraint, abortion. Without them population grows geometrically, resources arithmetically.
  • Mismatch → haves & have-nots; nature applies positive checks: starvation, epidemics, scarcity wars.
  • Value: started theory-building; anti-natalist policies everywhere raise marriage age.
Cross-check: notes say 1789 – the Essay was published in 1798.
Criticism & Marxian concept
  • Technological: population booms came with innovation; starvation deaths fell since 1950s → Neo-Malthusian view (control still urgent).
  • Sociological: ignores biological limits of fertility; mixes need for sex with need for children.
  • Economic – Karl Marx: not population but evils of capitalism divide society. Rich own means of production and profit; poor, underpaid, multiply their only asset – children. Remedy: fair, socialist economy.
  • UNFPA demographic variables: functional (fertility, mortality, migration), structural (size, distribution, composition).

Demographic transition model

Basics
  • Also "vital revolution". Thompson (1929) and Notestein (1945); based on European experience.
  • Assumptions: fertility & mortality fall from high to low; mortality falls first; socio-economic change accompanies.
  • Original 3 parts – stagal transition, causative components, predictive elements. Peter Haggett (1970) added predictive part → 5 stages.
  • Natural increase % = (BR − DR)/1000 × 100. TFR replacement level ≈ 2.1.
Five stages
StageFeaturesExamples (2010-11)
I High stationaryHigh fertility, fluctuating high mortality, high IMRPrimitive "fourth world" communities
II Early expandingMortality falls, fertility high → explosion; CBR >40, TFR >4.5Almost all Africa, Afghanistan, Yemen (Niger, Somalia CBR ~50)
III Late expandingFertility starts falling; CBR 20–40; TFR 2.5–4.5Most of Asia, Latin America, N Africa, S Africa
IV Low stationaryBoth low; CBR <20, TFR near replacementNew World; anti-natalist China, Kazakhstan, Turkey, Uruguay
V DecliningFertility below mortality; TFR ≤1.8; depopulationMost of Europe

Causes: Trewartha & Chung – man is biological (similar everywhere) and cultural (differs); economic development is the best contraceptive.

World population: growth & distribution

Growth
  • 10,000 BP (palaeolithic start) to 1750s – nearly constant (stage I); 1750–1950 slow rise; after 1950 exponential – J-curve.
  • With technology – S-curve / homeostatic plateau (neo-Malthusian): green, medical, IT revolutions.
  • UN 2010-11: just over 7 billion, ~2.6 lakh births/day; stability ~2150 at ~11 billion; >95% of growth in Africa, then South Asia.
Distribution & density
  • >75% people on <10% land; ~30% land uninhabited. 10 countries hold >60%: China, India, USA, Indonesia, Brazil, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Japan, Russia, Nigeria.
  • Avg density 41/km²: Asia 108, Europe 101 (dense); Africa 21, Latin America 20, Anglo-America 14, Oceania 3.
  • Ecumene (>100/km²): E Asia, S Asia (extensive, rural – China, India, Pakistan, Bangladesh); E Anglo-America (Great Lakes), W Europe, Japan (intensive, industrial).
  • Non-ecumene (<10/km²): equatorial forest, tropical & polar deserts, high mountains. Sporadic ecumene: extensive – Java, Mekong delta, Mesopotamia, Turan plains; intensive – Kiruna (iron), Magadan, Manaus, Namib (uranium), Persian Gulf, Sakhalin, ports (Honolulu, Singapore, Port Said, Suez, Panama, Colon), highland capitals (Addis Ababa, Kampala, Nairobi, Sana'a, La Paz).

Population composition – age-sex pyramids

  • Age-sex pyramid (19th century): young, mature, old vertically; male/female horizontally. Groups = cohorts.
  • Developing cohort: broad base, short (low life expectancy), fewer old women. Exponential – true pyramid (Kenya); stable growth – bulging middle (India).
  • Developed cohort: narrow, tall, more old women. Stable – balanced (Austria); declining – inverted (Sweden).
  • Composition reveals dividend (workforce), dependency ratio, gender inequality.

Population problems & policies

Over-population & anti-natalism
  • Over-population (more people than resources) → anti-natalism; under-population → pro-natalism.
  • KAP (knowledge, application, practice) – UN, early 1950s; failed: academic, no targets, distrust of ex-colonial powers.
India's anti-natalism (since 1952)
PhaseFeatures

China & pro-natalism
  • China (1982): after Maoist pro-natalism; constitutional one-child norm; success in 20 yrs – ended individual choice, PHC network, monitoring, sanctions. Criticism: future workforce scarcity, Tibetan resistance, poorest sex ratio; from 2000 minorities allowed two children; growth ~5%/decade vs India ~17%.
  • Under-population – Europe: ageing, below-replacement fertility, workforce shortage. Pro-natalism from 1960s (abortion made illegal – failed); from mid-1970s voluntary + incentives; France extreme (child-friendly towns, flexible hours, paid maternity/paternity leave) – still failed; Germany eased visas for skilled migrants.

India's growth third highest among populous countries (Nigeria 26%, Pakistan 24%); world ~12%. India = 17.5% of world – equal to USA + Indonesia + Brazil + Pakistan + Bangladesh + Japan. >50% in six states: UP, Maharashtra, Bihar, WB, AP, MP.

Density (2011: 382/km²)
  • Dense: all UTs except A&N – NCT ~11,000, Chandigarh ~9,000; Puducherry, Lakshadweep, Daman & Diu >2,000; DNH ~700. States: Bihar ~1,100 (densest), UP; also WB, Kerala, Punjab, Haryana, Assam, TN, Jharkhand, Goa.
  • Sparse: J&K, Sikkim, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, A&N (46), Arunachal (17 – lowest).
  • Moderate: intra-state contrast – alluvial plains of AP, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat vs plateaus; W Rajasthan vs Chambal; Mumbai–Pune, Mangalore–Bangalore urban belts.

Demographic dividend, sex ratio, GII & literacy

Demographic dividend
  • 0–6 yrs = 13% (2011); working-age to be ~65% by 2020–22. Low dependency in TN, Karnataka, Gujarat; Bihar, UP, MP will gain later. Productivity peaks at 40–49; half the labour-force addition (2011–31) aged 30–49. China's working cohort shrinking.
Sex ratio & GII
  • Sex ratio 940 (2011) – highest since 1971; world 984; China 926 lower; Russia, Japan, USA, Brazil above equity.
  • 1951: 11 units above equity; 2011: only Kerala & Puducherry. Peninsular states higher; lowest – Haryana 877 (state), Daman & Diu 618 (UT).
  • Child sex ratio 914 – lowest since 1971; no north Indian state above 900; J&K largest fall; even Kerala, Maharashtra declined; NE states welcome girl child.
  • GII dimensions: reproductive health (MMR India 200 vs China 37; adolescent fertility India 75 vs China 9), empowerment (women MPs ~11% vs China ~22%; secondary education 25+), labour market (LFPR).
  • Schemes: SABLA (girls 11–18; nutrition & non-nutrition), JSSK (free institutional delivery), IGMSY (cash ₹4,000 for first 2 births, women 19+), ICDS (oldest; children up to 6 + mothers), STEP (mid-1980s women's skills; ₹16,000/head, 90:10).
Literacy
  • Crude literacy = literates/total; effective = literates 7+/population 7+ (used since 1981). ~52% (1991) → 74% (2011); target 85% (11th Plan).
  • >85%: Kerala, Lakshadweep, Mizoram, NCT, Chandigarh. <70%: UP, Bihar, AP, MP, Rajasthan etc. Male–female gap 24% → <16%; gap >20% in Rajasthan, Bihar, MP, Chhattisgarh, J&K.

Migration theories

Basics & Ravenstein
  • Migration = change of residence (not commuting/circulation). Push at origin, pull at destination – real or perceived.
  • Ravenstein (notes: N Atlantic flows 1885–89): most migrate short distance (distance decay); economic cause main; adults more than families; women short, men long distance; big towns grow by immigration; every current has a counter-current; migration step-by-step; attraction ∝ size, ∝ 1/distance² (gravity).
Lee & Zelinsky
  • Everett Lee: four factors – origin, destination, intervening obstacles, personal. Destination pull must beat attachment to origin and obstacles; applies to all except push-driven migration.
  • Zelinsky (1971) mobility transition: links migration to demographic transition at 4 scales – international, regional, rural–urban, urban–urban. International & regional peak after early expanding stage, then fall (strict immigration rules; regional development); rural–urban lasts longer; urban–urban keeps rising.

Indian migration
  • Census: till 1961 by birthplace; 1971 adds last residence; 1981 adds reasons. NSSO data too. ~25% people are migrants (notes); economy main cause.
  • Streams: rural–rural most dominant (intra: Awadh–Rohilkhand → deltaic plain, Marusthali → Banas; inter: UP, Bihar → Punjab, Haryana, Assam). Rural–urban dominant after independence, 2nd since 1981 (UP, Bihar, MP → megacities; Maharashtra, NCT, Chandigarh destinations). Urban–urban 3rd – top-heavy pattern. Urban–rural – retirees, counter-current.
  • Pattern: sources UP & Bihar; destinations NCT & Maharashtra; peninsular states stable; isolated – northern mountains, Thar, Rann of Kutch. WB losing attraction, Assam gaining.
