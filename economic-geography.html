Von Thünen's agricultural location model (1826)

Assumptions & land use
  • German economist-farmer; book "The Isolated State" – self-sufficient state, no outside trade.
  • Assumptions: isotropic plain, equal fertility; farmers rational profit-maximisers; transport cost rises with distance & weight; water transport cheapest; land value falls away from centre.
  • Rings: 1 market gardening, 2 wood (heavy, so near), 3 intensive crops, 4 dairy (transitional), 5 three-field system, 6 extensive grazing. A navigable river turns rings into belts along it; secondary node possible.
Economic rent & applications
  • P = V − (E + T): V market value and E labour cost constant; T (transport cost) decides rent. Near market many profitable choices; far away fewer.
  • Applications: every city has a market-gardening ring; land value falls outward; transport cost = variable cost by distance & weight; heavy goods produced near market; water transport cheapest.

Weber's least-cost industrial location (1909) & later models

Weber's three tools
  • Isotropic plain, demand & price constant, rational entrepreneur seeks least-cost site; transport cost by distance & weight.
  • Locational triangle: base = raw-material sources, apex = market. Weight-losing (metallurgy) → near raw material; weight-gaining (assembly) → near market.
  • Isodapanes: lines of equal additional transport cost; with cheap labour the site can lie outside the triangle. Critical isodapane – labour saving = extra transport cost.
  • Agglomeration: overlap of critical isodapanes; primary (3 overlap) > secondary (2 overlap).
Appraisal & later scholars
  • Hoover: transport cost also by processing level (variable cost); fixed/terminal cost; minimise terminals; truck cheapest for short, rail medium, ship long distances.
  • Isard: substitution; agglomeration diseconomies; substituting raw material keeps profit – decentralised growth.
  • Smith: spatial margins of profitability – space-cost & space-revenue curves; profitable where revenue > cost.
  • Classical models can't explain modern global, environmental, labour-union and policy issues.

Industrial regions of the world

Four clusters: Anglo-American, West European, East European, East Asian (China alone in "modern" phase; others classical).

Anglo-American
  • Great Lakes, Appalachian resources, N Atlantic route. Complexes: New England (Boston; textiles → copper), Pittsburgh–Erie (iron-steel; Appalachian coal; Cleveland apparel), Mid-Atlantic (megalopolis – Washington, New York, Annapolis, Philadelphia; Baltimore, Dover steel & petrochemicals), Detroit (automobiles), Michigan (Chicago – food, meat packing, farm machinery), Lake Peninsula (Canada, >90% of its manufacturing – Montreal, Quebec, Ottawa, Toronto, Sudbury, London).
  • Great Lakes route Duluth → Montreal: Soo canal, Detroit, Welland (8 locks), St Lawrence.
Europe
  • West Europe (raw materials exhausting; N Atlantic & Suez routes, river waterways): Great Britain (London; Birmingham, Manchester; Sheffield; Liverpool, Leeds textiles); Lower Saxony (Hamburg, Bremen, Amsterdam, Rotterdam; Berlin food; Leipzig, Dresden optics; Kiel canal → Copenhagen dairy); Ruhr (metallurgy; Nancy, Strasbourg, Essen, Cologne, Dortmund). Canals: Mittelland, Dortmund–Ems, Rhine–Rhone, du Midi, Burgundy, Main, Kiel; Volga–Don.
  • East Europe (ex-USSR): Moscow–St Petersburg (turbines – St Petersburg; Nizhny Novgorod, Kursk metallurgy; Tver linen), Volga (Volgograd food & oil; Saratov, Kazan, Samara), Ural (Magnitogorsk steel; Orsk, Chelyabinsk, Yekaterinburg; Perm, Ufa petrochemicals).
Cross-check: Ruhr coal is mainly bituminous (coking), not anthracite as notes say.
East Asia
  • Japan (no raw materials): Honshu – Kwanto (Tokyo; Yokohama ships, Kawasaki petrochemicals, Chiba steel from US scrap), Nobi (Nagoya – "Detroit of Japan"; Gifu), Kinki (Osaka – "Manchester of Japan", Kobe, Kyoto); Kyushu (Kita-Kyushu, Nagasaki).
  • China: Manchurian (Shenyang, Anshan, Fushun), Beijing–Tianjin, Gansu corridor (Lanzhou, Luoyang), Chang Jiang (Shanghai, Wuhan); Grand Canal.

Agro-based industries: textiles

Cotton
  • Order: cotton, silk, jute, wool. Modern cotton mills from 1850s – Mumbai, Thane, Kalyan; raw material then market orientation; cheap labour, water.
  • Maharashtra – Mumbai "Cottonopolis", Thane, Kalyan, Nashik, Jalgaon, Akola, Amravati, Nagpur, Pune, Satara, Kolhapur, Sangli, Solapur. Gujarat – Ahmedabad "Manchester of North India", Vadodara, Bharuch, Nadiad, Rajkot, Surendranagar. TN – Coimbatore "Manchester of South India", Tiruppur, Madurai, Tirunelveli, Chennai. Others in UP, WB, MP, Karnataka.
  • India 2nd after China. Cotton Technology Mission (4 mini-missions: production, procurement, processing, marketing); TUFS (1999), SITP (2005).
  • Challenges: low labour productivity, erratic power, weather; prospects – exports (limits on China), retail (Reliance, Future, Walmart); rivals China, USA, Turkey, Pakistan, Thailand, Taiwan, Vietnam, Bangladesh.
Jute & wool
  • Jute ("golden fibre"): mid-19th century WB; raw-material oriented; >90% within ~100 km of Hooghly – Kolkata, Howrah, Murshidabad, Haldia, Barrackpore, Chinsurah, Titagarh. Others: Guntur, Srikakulam, Gorakhpur, Azamgarh, Katihar, Cuttack, Guwahati. Jute Technology Mission; "fibre of the millennium", geo-textiles; JNPT packaging pilot.
  • Wool: minor; Kanpur oldest & largest, Mumbai (imported wool); hosiery Punjab & Haryana (Amritsar, Panipat); carpets Srinagar, Jaipur, Bhopal, Warangal; SFURTI.
  • SITP parks: Tiruppur, Kanchipuram, Thiruvananthapuram, Vizag, Bangalore, Surat, Ludhiana, Kanpur. Mega-clusters (PPP, up to ₹70 cr): handloom – Varanasi, Murshidabad, Sivasagar; powerloom – Bhiwandi, Erode.

Sugar, paper, leather, food processing & MSMEs

Sugar
  • Modern mills early 20th century in UP & Bihar; decentralised after 1960s; raw-material oriented. MH + UP >60%. Maharashtra leads sugar (tropical climate, higher sucrose, longer crushing, cooperatives) – Pune, Nashik, Satara, Kolhapur, Ahmednagar, Aurangabad; UP leads cane but lower sucrose, shorter crushing.
  • India 2nd after Brazil. Problems: decontrol after dual-price regime, cyclic surplus/deficit, grower–miller disputes, essential-commodity control orders. Proposal: mills as mini energy complexes (molasses ethanol – MNRE).
Paper, leather, food
  • Paper: Titagarh (WB) early 20th century; paper & paperboard – AP, MH, UP; strawboard (bamboo) – Guwahati; newsprint – Nepanagar (1956), Mokokchung, Kottayam; security paper – Mysore, Nainital, Hoshangabad. Delicensed, 100% FDI (1997); fragmented capacity.
  • Leather: TN (Dindigul, Madurai) & UP (Kanpur, Agra) >60% of tanning.
  • Food processing: primary (near production, cold storage – NHM, aquaculture) and secondary compound (drying, canning – market/port clusters).
MSMEs
  • MSMED Act 2006; reserved items; micro/small/medium by investment. ~90% of units (notes); rural >60%. TN most registered units; Maharashtra largest employer; TN, MH, AP, UP, WB >50%.
  • PM's Task Force 2010: credit, skills & marketing. CGFS (SIDBI, NABARD, PSBs; collateral-free; priority women, SC/ST); PMEGP; RGUMY (first-generation entrepreneurs); SFURTI (oldest, cottage industries).

Chemical industries: fertiliser, pharma, cement

Fertiliser
  • Sindri (1951) first; raw-material oriented – naphtha, natural gas, coal. Pipelines: Digboi–Barauni, Salaya–Mathura (IOCL), HBJ (GAIL).
  • FCIL (1961) oldest/largest: Talcher, Ramagundam (Sindri, Gorakhpur closed); FAGMIL gypsum (Bikaner, Nagaur, Jodhpur, Barmer). NFL (urea & phosphate; profitable): Nangal, Panipat, Bathinda. RCF Mumbai (gas); MFL Chennai (first JV, Iranian oil co.); FACT Kochi; PPL Paradip; PPCL; BVFCL Namrup (youngest). HFL Durgapur, Barauni closed.
  • Retention price scheme → urea procurement system; Fertiliser Monitoring System; Nutrient Based Subsidy.
Pharmaceuticals
  • Market oriented. HAL (Hindustan Antibiotics, 1954, Pimpri) oldest; JVs MAPL Nagpur, KAPL Bangalore, MSDPL Imphal. IDPL (1961) largest – Rishikesh, Gurgaon, Hyderabad; subsidiaries IDPL-TN Chennai, Bihar Drugs Muzaffarpur; JVs Odisha (Bhubaneswar), Rajasthan (Jaipur). Kolkata – Bengal Chemicals, Bengal Immunity, Smith Stanistreet.
  • Pharma Policy 2002: 20-yr patents, delicensing, progressive pricing → Ranbaxy, Cipla, Biocon, Dabur etc. Later compulsory licensing; NPPA's National Pharma Pricing Policy 2012 caps essential drug prices.
Cement
  • Early 20th century Bihar & TN; decontrol 1989. Raw materials – limestone, dolomite, slag, sludge, shells. ~75% from MP (Rewa, Satna, Katni, Jabalpur), AP (Kurnool, Cuddapah), Chhattisgarh (Raipur, Bilaspur, Bhilai), Rajasthan (Kota, Bundi, Sawai Madhopur), Gujarat (Okha, Dwarka, Porbandar, Jamnagar).
  • India 2nd after China; makes OPC, blast-furnace, oil-well, white cement; cheapest ex-factory price but high handling cost.

Metallurgical industries

Iron & steel
  • Raw-material oriented – Dharwar belt (Chota Nagpur, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka).
  • TISCO Jamshedpur (1907) oldest; new plant in Odisha (notes: near Dhamra port, Jajpur). Kulti (1919) + Hirapur, Burnpur → IISCO (nationalised 1972). Bhadravati (1923) – Kudremukh ore, Chitradurga Mn, Hospet limestone, SCCL coal.
  • HSL: Rourkela, Durgapur, Bhilai (Bailadila, Dalli-Rajhara ore; Balaghat Mn; Korba coal) → SAIL; Bokaro ended first phase. Second phase: Salem, Vijayanagar, Vizag (first port-based). Port-based new: POSCO Paradip, Ratnagiri. Mini-steel: Delhi, Jaipur, Indore, Pune, Bangalore.
  • Challenges: depleting ore, no coking coal; MMTC curbs high-grade ore export. National Steel Policy 2005 – Durgapur long, Rourkela flat products; JSW, Essar; EIA delays.
Aluminium
  • From 2nd Plan; raw-material + cheap power (>60% of cost is electricity).
  • Public: NALCO (Angul, Koraput – largest/youngest), BALCO; notes list INDALCO (Hirakud). Private: HINDALCO Renukoot (Aditya Birla), BALCO Korba & MALCO Mettur (Sterlite).
  • Uses: power transmission, aircraft, rail coaches, defence, space; delicensed 1991. Challenges: coal links, law & order in bauxite areas, power shortage.

Transport: roads, maritime, waterways

Roads
  • MoRTH. Primary (NH ~2% length, big share of traffic), secondary (SH, district – states), rural (~80%), border roads (BRO since 1960s, 15,200 km frontier).
  • 1998–99 policy: BOT, roads as industry, tax holidays, NHAI up to 40% funding.
  • NHDP: Golden Quadrilateral (NH 8, 4, 5, 2); N–S (Srinagar–Kanyakumari) & E–W (Silchar–Porbandar) ~7,000 km; port links NH 8A Kandla, 4A Mormugao, 48 Mangalore, 7A Tuticorin, 5A Paradip. PMBJY (2005, PPP, 14,000 km); PMRRP J&K (NH 1A, 1B, 1C, 1D); RRP LWE states (~5,500 km); SARDP-NE (~7,000 km); PMGSY rural.
Maritime & waterways
  • Shipping Corporation of India; Cochin Shipyard largest; Hindustan Shipyard (Vizag), Hooghly Dock; Garden Reach oldest; Mazagaon, Goa Shipyard; private L&T, Bharati.
  • Major ports ~75% trade (Major Port Trusts). West (natural, Suez route): Kochi (lagoon), Mangalore, Mormugao (Zuari mouth), JNPT (first BOT), Kandla (Gulf of Kutch), Mumbai (largest hinterland). East (artificial, Malacca route): Chennai oldest artificial, Vizag deepest, Kolkata–Haldia riverine (largest eastern hinterland), Ennore first corporate port, Paradip, Tuticorin.
  • Minor ports ≤25%; coastline 7,516 km; ~200 minor (~170 only on paper); Maharashtra most, Gujarat Maritime Board most efficient.
  • IWAI (1986): NW1 Allahabad–Haldia (~1,600 km), NW2 Brahmaputra (~890 km, Dibrugarh–Dhubri per notes), NW3 West Coast Canal (~200 km, Kollam–Kottapuram), NW4 Kakinada–Puducherry, NW5 East Coast Canal + Brahmani–Mahanadi.
Cross-check: NW2 is Sadiya–Dhubri (891 km); NW3 ends at Kottapuram (notes: Kottayam); NW4 is ~1,095 km (notes ~1,900).

Indian trade, SEZ & export promotion

  • Exports: primary share falling, manufactures rising; gems & jewellery, petroleum products up. Imports: electronics & capital goods down; gold, silver, crude up.
  • Direction: EU & USA share falling; Asia & Africa rising; >60% trade with China, UAE, USA, Saudi, Switzerland, Singapore, Germany, Hong Kong, Indonesia, Iraq, Japan, Belgium, S Korea, Kuwait, Nigeria. Agreements: CEPA Japan, CEPA S Korea, BIMSTEC.
  • States: MH, GJ, TN, AP, KA ~65%; Punjab, Odisha, Rajasthan growing. ASIDE scheme (12th Plan, NE focus).
  • EPZ 1960s – Kandla first in Asia; failed (controls, infrastructure, fiscal). SEZ Act 2005 (in force 2006): duty-free enclaves; EPZs Kandla, Surat, Kochi, Chennai, Vizag, Santacruz, Falta, Noida converted.
  • FTP measures 2012-13: EPCG (25% obligation for NE/backward areas), green technology products (16; 75% obligation), SPILIS (10% scrips labour-intensive), market & product diversification, e-BRC.

Dualism & determinism (thought)

  • Dualisms: human vs physical, contemporary vs historical, regional vs systematic, idiographic vs nomothetic, determinism vs possibilism (only ideological one).
  • Determinism phases: environmentalism → classical → social Darwinism → neo-determinism → social/cultural.
  • Environmentalism: Hecataeus (father of geography), Herodotus, Strabo (17 volumes; Europe, Asia, Libya), Eratosthenes & Aristotle (climate and habitability); Ibn Khaldun (Muqaddimah) – first environmental determinist; Kant.
  • Classical: Humboldt (empirical inductive – similar nature, similar response) and Ritter (empirical deductive – anthropocentric "unity in diversity").
